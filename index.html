Civic Exhaustion Is a System Design Failure

Democracy is grinding down not because citizens don't care, but because participation has become an unsustainable personal tax. This problem situation represents the single most urgent, foundational friction in this Build Cycle.

01 / ATTENTION

The Collapse of Everyday Participation

Across local councils, school boards, and elections, citizens are withdrawing in unprecedented numbers. The problem isn't voter apathy—it's that participating in civic life today feels like taking on an unguided second job. When well-meaning citizens try to engage, they encounter black-hole communication loops, opaque administrative processes, and toxic media environments that burn them out.

02 / INTEREST

48 Signals Expose Two Deep System Fault Lines

Synthesizing 48 raw friction signals from real constituents, election workers, and grassroots leaders reveals two structural dynamics:

  • The Un-Scaffolded Tax: Everyday democratic maintenance relies on unpaid labor without playbooks, turning civic duty into an exhausting privilege.
  • The Broken Covenant: Institutions extract public sentiment without offering true accountability or visible impact, turning citizens into decorative inputs.
03 / DESIRE

The Most Impactful Problem We Can Solve

If we ignore this problem situation, every civic tool we build will fail to gain traction because it will be feeding into an already burnt-out user base. By choosing to tackle this problem situation, our research pod will step back to saturate in these structural dynamics, diagnosing the underlying paradoxes before building solutions that actually unlock human agency.
